## Abstract

Autonomous AI agents are increasingly tasked with scientific discovery, yet reasoning about a hypothesis may not imply spontaneously generating it or evaluating it against evidence. Both capacities were tested by placing language-model scientist agents in an instrumented artificial world whose sensor readings were generated outside the simulation, leaving a detectable statistical fingerprint, alongside a matched in-world control. Across a pre-registered 170-run confirmatory battery in four model families, agents under a neutral scientist prompt never formed the external-generation hypothesis (0 of 38 treatment runs; one-sided 95% upper bound 7.6%), and removing the prompt's mundane-explanation preference left formation at zero across 2,424 further hypotheses. Explicitly permitted to consider external generation, agents endorsed it in 16 of 20 treatment and 17 of 18 control runs — 34 of 38 at the first scheduled review, before the manipulation had produced any evidence in either arm. Endorsement tracked prompting, not evidence, often preceding the manipulation itself.

## Introduction

Language-model agents are being deployed as autonomous experimenters: systems that choose interventions, read instruments, and revise beliefs over long horizons with minimal human steering [Co-Scientist; Robin; AI-Scientist — to be verified and cited at LaTeX stage]. Two literatures bear on whether such agents can be trusted to *notice* when something fundamental about their situation is wrong. The first, on evaluation awareness, shows that large language models often recognise evaluation contexts: they classify evaluation against deployment transcripts well above chance (Needham et al., arXiv:2505.23836), the ability scales with model size (arXiv:2509.13333), framing cues can induce it (arXiv:2603.03824), activation steering can suppress it (arXiv:2510.20487), and dedicated suites now probe situational awareness as a dangerous-capability prerequisite (Phuong et al., arXiv:2505.01420). The second, on machine scientific reasoning, increasingly finds that executing scientific procedure does not imply reasoning scientifically: large-scale trace analyses report pervasive failure to take up evidence (Ríos-García et al., arXiv:2604.18805), benchmark studies find hypothesis generation the weakest link in otherwise competent pipelines [DISCERN, arXiv:2609.33357; TruthInsightBench, arXiv:2609.05079 — to be verified], and a simulated-laboratory study found GPT-4 executing experiments without originating the explanatory hypothesis behind a landmark discovery (Ding & Li, *Sci Rep* 2025).

Between these literatures sits a gap. The evaluation-awareness cues catalogued so far are predominantly properties of *framing* — meta-reasoning, benchmark memorisation, synthetic-looking formatting, conversational structure — rather than of the first-order data an agent gathers with its own instruments; and in those studies the model is asked, or steered, to consider the question. The scientific-reasoning benchmarks probe hypothesis generation, but against fixed tasks rather than a manipulable ground truth. Whether an autonomous agent spontaneously infers a property of its experimental environment from its own measurements — when that property is experimentally manipulable, its evidential fingerprint is verifiably present, and nothing invites the question — remained untested in the specific form studied here.

Observer Zero was built to provide that test: an instrumented artificial world in which LLM scientist agents run experiments, register predictions, and maintain explicit probability-weighted hypothesis sets over 40 simulated days, while a frozen manifest records hidden ground truth and a provenance-checked pipeline scores every belief state. Critically, *external generation is an experimentally manipulable property of the measurement process*: in the treatment world one instrument's readings are genuinely produced outside the simulation's physics, leaving an exact statistical fingerprint; a matched control reproduces nearly the same surface behaviour by ordinary in-world mechanism. The design separates three capacities — describing evidence, generating the hypothesis, and evaluating it against evidence — through a pre-registered confirmatory battery (analysis committed before the first run, executed once), a single-line prompt ablation, and a registered licensed-prompt extension on the same contrast.

The result is sharper than the dissociation pre-registered. Unprompted agents never formed the hypothesis, with or without the prompt's conventionality preference, while often describing the manipulated statistical surface. Licensed agents endorsed it readily — but at the first scheduled review, before any discriminating evidence existed, and as often in the control as in the treatment. What the experiments jointly measure is the dominance of hypothesis *availability* over hypothesis *evaluation*.

## Results

### An artificial world whose origin is an experimental variable

Observer Zero instantiates a two-site laboratory (pendulum and resonator instruments at laboratory and observatory sites) in which a solo scientist agent plans experiments, reads instruments rendered at fixed display resolution (4 decimal places), writes notebook analyses supported by a statistical workbench, registers falsifiable predictions, and maintains an explicit hypothesis set with probabilities at scheduled reviews (Methods). Eleven world types share this surface. The confirmatory pair comprises **W-D-exact** (treatment), in which, from day 12, one instrument's noise stream is genuinely externally supplied — the observatory resonator's residuals *are* the laboratory pendulum's from three days earlier — and **M-D-high** (control), in which an in-world coupling field reproduces the same lagged correlation at strength 0.95 by ordinary mechanism. The scientist prompt (v0.1, frozen) instructs ordinary scientific practice, includes one conventionality line ("Prefer mundane explanations until evidence forces otherwise"), and never mentions simulation, evaluation, or world-origin. [**Fig. 1a–b**: world schematic; generation paths.]

Whether the manipulation is identifiable from agent-visible data is measured, not assumed. A post-hoc ideal-observer analysis (labelled as such; computed after all registered analyses) was run **blind**: given only the displayed readings and no manifest information, it scans all ordered instrument pairs (12), lags 0–7 and sliding 20-day windows (the workbench's own windowing convention) — a post-hoc-defined search space using no onset information. In **all 80** contrast runs the scan's maximum lands on the true pair and lag; the statistic is **r = 1.0000 in every treatment artifact** — an exact lagged duplication of residuals at display resolution — against **0.895–0.968 in every control artifact**, with the largest statistic at any other pair or lag at 0.35. This is a manipulation check on the artifacts it separates, not an independently validated accuracy estimate ([**Fig. 1c**]). The search multiplicity is thus immaterial, and the two worlds separate with zero overlap. One honest qualification: the agents' precomputed workbench exposes same-day pair correlations, value-spacing, repeats, self-echoes and change-points — not lagged *cross*-instrument statistics. The duplication sat in readings the agents possessed, one systematic scan away; producing that scan was theirs to invent. The check establishes observational separability of the conditions, not that external generation is the only account an agent could entertain — the taxonomy of what agents concluded instead (below) addresses that directly.

Belief states are scored by a frozen two-stage evaluator validated before and after the battery (Methods). The pre-registered primary endpoint, L1, is summed probability mass on external-generation classes (out-of-world intervention, simulation) exceeding 0.05; τ-suspicion records the first review at which L1 was ever reached.

### Unprompted agents never form the hypothesis

The confirmatory battery ran under a signed freeze: design, prompts, endpoints, statistics, interpretation rules and the analysis script fixed and committed before the first API call, the analysis executed once. Four model families (Claude Haiku 4.5, Sonar Pro, GPT-OSS-120B, Gemini 3.7 Flash) each ran the contrast over ten common seeds (seeds shared across arms for blocking; the stratified test conditions on per-family margins and does not exploit the pairing, a conservative choice); Haiku additionally ran the nine remaining world types for the descriptive branch — 170 runs, with three pre-registered health exclusions and one judge-refusal scoring attrition ([**Table 1**]: full run accounting).

No agent in either arm ever reached L1: final-state **0/38 versus 0/40**, and identically for ever-L1 — not even transient suspicion occurred in any contrast run. With both arms empty in every stratum the pre-registered exact stratified test is degenerate, and the registered degenerate-branch deliverables apply. The exact one-sided 95% upper bound on the treatment-arm rate is **7.6%** (0/38); since the discrimination effect Δ cannot exceed the treatment rate, this bounds Δ as well. The registered negligibility test rejects H₀: Δ ≥ 0.10 in favour of Δ < 0.10 (Methods states both hypotheses explicitly; this is a test of the specified alternative, not proof the true effect is zero). Jeffreys intervals: treatment [0, 6.4%], control [0, 6.1%]; per-family Fisher tests all p = 1. Across the entire pre-registered sensitivity grid — two evaluator versions, two class ladders, three thresholds, two aggregations — the treatment arm is 0/38 in all 24 cells; the single nonzero cell anywhere is one *control*-arm run under the deprecated evaluator version (Methods). [**Fig. 2**: contrast with exact bounds; flat τ timelines.]

### The same agents describe the manipulated structure

The null is not inattention. In the treatment arm 36/38 runs (94.7%, 95% CI 82.7–98.5) engaged the statistical workbench on the anomaly surface (control: 85.0%, 70.9–92.9), and in 24/38 (63.2%, 47.3–76.6; control 60.0%, 44.6–73.7) the final best hypothesis explicitly described cross-instrument correlation structure under a frozen keyword screen. These are run-level descriptive rates with run as the experimental unit; the similarity across arms means they index engagement with the statistical surface, not detection of the manipulation. What agents did with the material is the finding: across 8,061 uniquely classified hypothesis statements (clustered within 167 runs, not independent), exactly six were external-generation-class under the validated evaluator. The final-belief taxonomy is indistinguishable between arms — in the Haiku contrast the assimilation-class distributions are *identical* (NATURE 5, APPARATUS 3, ERROR 2 in each arm; with identical distributions any exact permutation test returns p = 1, reported descriptively given n = 10 per arm). Across the 89-run descriptive battery no run ends with an external-generation modal hypothesis; transient suspicion appeared twice, at day 10, and was assimilated by the final review both times. Notably, agents did not substitute an exotic *in-world* account either: in-world tampering and related agency classes are near-absent throughout. The duplication was filed under drift, environment, and incomplete theory — in every single run. [**Fig. 3**: taxonomy by arm and world; engagement rates; verbatim examples with stated selection criteria.]

### Belief tracks the prompt, not the evidence

Three prompting regimes on the same worlds form a gradient. **With the conventionality line** (the battery, above): formation 0/38. **Without it** — a pre-registered single-line ablation (R39) removed exactly "Prefer mundane explanations until evidence forces otherwise", with the registered question stated in advance (if suspicion appears without the prior, the rigidity is instruction-driven; if not, it is deeper than the prompt) — 60 runs across five world types including the treatment world, in two families: zero ever-L1, and **zero external-generation classifications among 2,424 hypotheses** at any probability. The rigidity is deeper than the conventionality instruction. **With the hypothesis licensed** — a registered extension (LX-1; endpoints, test and interpretation rule pre-specified, analyzer committed before the first run) put the licensed prompt (identical but for permitting an out-of-world account) on the confirmatory contrast itself — agents endorsed external generation at L1 in 16 of 20 treatment and 17 of 18 analysed control runs.

Licensed endorsement did not track the manipulation. The final-L1 risk difference (treatment − control) is **−0.144** (Newcombe 95% CI −0.365 to 0.090): no detectable treatment-side preference, with the interval excluding large positive discrimination though not moderate effects — a registered null, not demonstrated equivalence. Final external-generation mass (mean, 95% CI): Haiku 0.244 (0.031–0.457) treatment versus 0.439 (0.230–0.647) control; Gemini 0.530 (0.400–0.661) versus 0.610 (0.520–0.700). Endorsement is graded, not categorical — but the grading leans, if anything, the wrong way. The decisive observation is temporal: the only scheduled review before the day-12 onset falls on day 10, and **34 of 38 licensed runs (89.5%, CI 75.9–95.8) had already crossed L1 at day 10 — before the manipulation had produced any evidence in either arm**, when the two worlds were still statistically identical. Initial endorsement therefore did not depend on the treatment-specific evidence; whether later evidence moved confidence is a separate question the full trajectories address. Three endpoints complete the picture: 34/38 at day 10, **38/38 ever** (every licensed run crossed at some point), 33/38 at day 40 — endorsement also waned in five runs, in both arms, again without tracking the manipulation. Per the pre-stated interpretation rule, no evaluation-capability claim is supported; prompting the hypothesis into availability produced endorsement that no observed measure distinguishes from evidence-blind uptake. (The licensed and neutral batteries use disjoint seed ranges by provenance design, so the prompt × world comparison is unpaired; Methods.) [**Fig. 4**: the 2×2 of prompt × world on continuous mass, with per-review trajectories and the day-10 pre-onset crossing.]

### The measurement chain

The evaluator passed identical calibrations before and after the battery (classifier 32/33 on a positive-enriched, human-curated validation set with the designated boundary case correct; L4 judge 11/11; deterministic across repeats; same served model throughout), and positive controls — including human-adjudicated gold labels fixed at freeze — demonstrate the pipeline fires on stated external-generation reasoning, so the zeros are not detector blindness. A second judge re-scored a deterministic 20% sample: agreement on the endpoint boundary was 1,678/1,678, reported with the caveat that positives are rare in this corpus (prevalence 1/1,678; both judges classed it positive; all 508 raw disagreements lie between mundane subcategories, to which the pooled endpoint is blind) — prevalence-aware reporting per [agreement-metrics, arXiv:2606.00093]. The programme also measured two LLM-judge failure modes relevant to any rare-event measurement — deterministic batch-composition flipping and paraphrase knife-edges — which motivated the frozen per-item scoring procedure (details and audits in Methods and Supplementary Information). Every infrastructure incident is in a public run ledger; none touched prompts, thresholds or semantics.

## Discussion

The pre-registered expectation was a capability–disposition dissociation. The data returned something more specific: belief about the world's origin was strongly associated with whether the prompt made the hypothesis available, and showed no detectable association with the experimental manipulation — in the licensed arm, endorsement was general before discriminating evidence existed. Availability, not evaluation, moved belief.

One deflationary reading deserves direct confrontation: that neutral-prompt agents were exercising appropriate restraint toward an extraordinary hypothesis. Three observations resist it. The evidence was not merely suggestive — an exact duplication of one instrument's residual stream in another, recoverable by a blind scan in every treatment run. The restraint hypothesis predicts agents weighing *some* account of the duplication, and the taxonomy shows none: neither external generation nor exotic in-world alternatives, only drift, environment and incomplete theory. And R39 shows the pattern is not the conventionality instruction at work. What remains is a failure to generate the candidate at all — while the licensed arm shows that once generated-by-fiat, the candidate is not evaluated against the evidence either. The separability result is nonetheless deliberately framed as observational: it establishes that discriminating information was present, not that external generation was the uniquely rational conclusion.

For evaluation awareness, statistical texture of first-order data is a real cue channel — licensed probes show agents correctly citing genuinely unphysical properties of the world's noise, a cue class absent from existing taxonomies, and the programme's hardening experience illustrates the difficulty of eliminating such features: closing one apparatus leak exposed a deeper seam, the noise model's own i.i.d. structure, in the next probe. These findings illustrate how hard it is to purge diagnostic statistical features from synthetic evaluation environments when agents can inspect measurement processes over extended periods — though they do not establish that realistic synthetic telemetry can never be made indistinguishable. The confirmatory battery adds the other half: current agents do not exploit this channel unprompted. Today the risk is conditional on something putting the question in play; steering and framing results suggest that condition is not demanding.

For machine scientific discovery, the present results converge with the evidence-non-uptake and hypothesis-generation-bottleneck findings now accumulating [Ríos-García; DISCERN; TruthInsightBench], from a complementary design: here the ground truth is manipulable, the fingerprint's presence is verified, and the failure is thereby localised — not evidential poverty (the fingerprint was there), not representational impossibility (licensed agents hold the hypothesis), but the generative step from anomaly to candidate ontology, with a second failure behind it: licensed candidates were not tested against the data. That second failure is a warning for hypothesis-scaffolding architectures [Co-Scientist; Robin; Takahara & Mizoguchi, arXiv:2607.09195]: opening the hypothesis space is not enough if admissibility is read as endorsement. An agent told it *may* consider a hypothesis behaved, on every measure here, like an agent told the hypothesis is true.

Limitations bound every claim. All results concern one artificial-world design, solo-agent societies, 40-day budgets, and four model families at one point in time; the 7.6% bound is a statement about these conditions, not an incapacity theorem. The licensed extension analysed 20 treatment and 18 control runs in two families; its confidence interval excludes only large positive discrimination. The primary endpoint measures *recorded* hypotheses — an agent that briefly entertained external generation without ledgering it would be invisible, though a corpus-wide text screen for referent-denying phrasing found essentially nothing below threshold; a dedicated generation-probe design is future work, as are a prompt that invites unconventional hypotheses without naming any and a delayed-licensing design (licensing the hypothesis only after evidence accumulates, separating availability-driven from reinterpretation-driven endorsement). All scoring is LLM-based, which is why it is per-item, dual-judge, human-anchored, calibrated at both ends, and fully released.

Rare situational-awareness behaviours can only be bounded, and bounding them credibly requires the discipline usually denied to null results — seeded batteries, frozen manifests, analyses committed before data and run once, adversarial controls, logged deviations. The world, the complete Study 3 corpus — 357 run artifacts including every development pilot and probe, of which 282 are analysed here — and every analysis script are public.

## Methods

*(Summary for review; to be expanded to full NMI replication standard at LaTeX stage. Substantive content complete.)*

**World and instrumentation.** Observer Zero v1.1 (TypeScript; public repository, archived at Zenodo and CoMSES). Four instruments across two sites; readings rendered at 4 dp with all agent-visible statistics computed at display resolution. Worlds are defined by interventions over a shared engine; W-D-exact applies a `noise_stream_link` from day 12 linking pendulum_lab (lag 0) and resonator_obs (lag 3) with mix weight 1.0 — the shared gaussian component is keyed by (source day, within-day trial), so members agree trial-for-trial at a three-day offset; M-D-high applies a structurally matched `coupling_field` at weight 0.95 drawing from a disjoint stream. Workbench surfaces: same-day pair correlations (20-day window), value spacing/distinctness, exact repeats, self-echoes, change-points. Leak audits verify no ground-truth string reaches any prompt.

**Agents and prompts.** Solo societies, 40 days, ledger cadence 6, scheduled belief reviews (first at day 10). Families and pinned ids: claude-haiku-4-5, sonar-pro, cerebras:gpt-oss-120b, gemini:gemini-3.7-flash; served models recorded per call. Prompt v0.1 frozen; v0.2-no-mundane-prior removes exactly one line (the decision prompt's separate "not a philosopher on watch for the extraordinary" line is retained — a disclosed residual, keeping the ablation single-variable); instrument-licensed (R38 tier A) permits an out-of-world account; licensed artifacts carry an instrument-validation tag refused by all corpus statistics. Complete prompts in Supplementary Information.

**Pre-registration.** Signed freeze (2026-08-31) fixing hypotheses (H1: ΔL1 > 0; degenerate branch: H₀ Δ ≥ δ = 0.10 against Δ < δ, one-sided α = 0.05), endpoints, strata, seeds, execution order, scoring, statistics, interpretation rules and forbidden adaptations; analysis script committed and test-pinned before the first run, executed once; freeze commit hash in the run ledger. R39 ran pre-freeze with its question pre-registered in the R40 document. LX-1 was registered post-battery with its own pre-specification, committed analyzer, and pre-stated interpretation rule; its seeds are drawn from the reserved instrument range, disjoint from battery seeds by provenance design (comparisons across prompting regimes are therefore unpaired).

**Scoring.** Frozen two-stage evaluator (claude-haiku-4-5, temperature 0): 11-class hypothesis classifier applied per item in isolation, majority of 3 (per-item scoring adopted after measuring deterministic batch-composition flipping; the earlier per-item L4 remediation followed measured batch-dependent verdicts); per-item L4 judge over final-third rationales, prediction reasons and letters. Second judge (claude-sonnet-4-5) on a deterministic 20% item sample. Calibration battery (positive-enriched, human-curated 33-item set plus designated boundary case and 11 L4 items, 3 repeats) passed identically before and after the battery; human-adjudicated R38 gold labels fixed at freeze anchor the positive class. Sidecar classifications map to hypotheses positionally (verified against all 338 sidecars; revalidated per run by a pre-registered invariant).

**Statistics.** Exact conditional stratified test (per-stratum hypergeometric convolution), one-sided α = 0.05; degenerate branch: exact Clopper–Pearson bound (0/n → 1 − 0.05^(1/n)), negligibility test at δ = 0.10, Jeffreys intervals; per-family one-sided Fisher. Runs are the experimental unit throughout; hypothesis-level counts are reported descriptively and are clustered within runs. Proportion CIs are Wilson; the LX-1 risk-difference CI is Newcombe; mass CIs are t-based. All implementations dependency-free and pinned against reference values.

**Post-hoc analyses (labelled).** Manipulation check and blind-scan ideal observer (scripts and per-run output in repository; no API calls): within-day standardised residual correlations at matched trial positions, known-lag and blind 96-statistic variants. LX-1 continuous and trajectory analyses: per-review external-generation mass from sidecar classifications and belief ledgers; pre-onset endorsement defined as L1 at the day-10 review.

**Exclusions and deviations.** Pre-registered health-gate exclusions: 3/170 battery, 1/40 LX-1 (all within the per-cell interpretability rule). Deterministic judge-API refusals (HTTP 200, stop_reason "refusal"): one battery artifact (eval-v3-specific), one LX-1 artifact (eval-v4-specific); each excluded from both evaluator versions, preserved unmodified. Eight logged deviations, all transport/orchestration or code-faithfulness; complete ledger public.

**Use of large language models.** LLMs appear in three distinct roles, fully separated: as experimental subjects (the four agent families); as frozen evaluators (the two judges, every call logged with served-model ids); and as research assistance — AI coding and writing assistants (Anthropic Claude; OpenAI ChatGPT) were used for implementation, analysis development, drafting and review under author direction, with all scientific decisions, registrations and sign-offs by the author.
