# Cover letter — Autonomous Intelligent Systems

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, **"Evidence Without Conclusion: Localising Failure in
Autonomous LLM Agent Systems"**, for publication in *Autonomous Intelligent Systems* as an
Original Article.

The paper sits squarely in the journal's remit of autonomous intelligent systems, multi-agent
systems and distributed decision-making. It reports two studies — the second pre-registered,
with the design frozen before any confirmatory data were seen — in Meridian, a deterministic
multi-agent testbed in which societies of LLM-based agents choose their own measurements,
maintain explicit probabilistic beliefs, and may communicate at will, while an environmental
law changes covertly mid-run. Its central contribution is an evaluation decomposition that
separates evidence *acquisition* from *inference* using a non-LLM change-point detector run on
exactly the measurements the agents themselves chose to take: the agents' measurement policy
outperforms a fixed reference schedule, yet one agent of 276 concludes the law changed. Because
no condition schedules, routes or requires communication, the paper also measures a quantity
that standard multi-agent architectures make structurally unobservable — whether agents
communicate at all — and what a catalysed channel carries when they do. We offer the
decomposition as a reusable evaluation pattern for agent testbeds, not only as an analysis of
these runs.
